Home birth or birth center for my first baby?

The short answer: If you love your home, and the birth center you've signed up with also does home births, wonderful. I think you should do it. Just know that birth is physical. There's some blood, and there will be some laundry. Your midwives will help clean up, but if you'd rather not have any of that in your house, choose the birth center. That's a great choice too.

By Sybille Mom of two · first birth in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

If you're choosing between a home birth and a birth center for your first baby, I want to start with a little celebration. You already know you have choices. I didn't, with my first. 💛

If you love your home, and the birth center you've signed up with also does home births, wonderful. I think you should do it. Just know that birth is physical. There's some blood, and there will be some laundry. Your midwives will help clean up, but if you'd rather not have any of that in your house, choose the birth center. That's a great choice too. For me, it was home. I love my house, my food, my room and the way it smells, and I wanted my family around me. I didn't want to go anywhere, not even a day or two later. I just wanted to stay. But I would have been perfectly happy at the birth center too. What mattered most was that my family and my midwives were there. A home birth also means there's nothing to pack and nowhere to drive. It's one less thing to think about. And with my second baby, I already had my daughter at home. I worried that if labor started at night, my husband would have to stay home with her, or we'd have to find someone to watch her. For me, it was just easier to do it at home.

What they have in common

Both a home birth and a birth center birth are usually led by midwives. Both are built around calm, undisturbed birth, with time to get to know your midwife during pregnancy. And both are planned with a clear way to get to a hospital if you need or want more help.

If no one around you has done either, you're not alone.

At home

Your midwife comes to you. You labor in your own space, in your own bed or a birth pool, eating your own snacks, with only the people you choose. For my second birth, that meant my own bed, a steak dinner (about five minutes later it was go time 🥩) and my first child there for the first part, so proud to help her mama, until it was time for her to go to bed.

At a birth center

A birth center is an inviting place you get to know really well, because you go there for your visits every month, so it feels a bit like a home away from home. It's beautiful and warm. Nobody rushes you, it's calm, and there are no harsh bright lights. And just like at home, you can invite whoever you want to your birth. Some moms love that it feels like home without being home: no laundry, no siblings to plan around, and a place that's set up just for birth.

A few questions to help you choose

  • Does your birth center also offer home births? Not all of them do.
  • Where do I picture myself feeling most relaxed when labor gets intense?
  • How do I feel about leaving home during labor, or about staying put?
  • How far is each option from my home, and from a hospital?
  • Who do I want with me, and is there room for them?
  • What does my insurance cover for each? Here's more on insurance.
  • How did I feel when I met the midwives at each place?
  • If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.

Visit a birth center if you can, and meet a home birth midwife or two. Your body will often tell you before your mind does.

Questions moms ask

Is a birth center or home birth better for a first baby?

Both are amazing choices. If you love your home and your birth center also does home births, I think you should do it. If you'd rather not have the blood and laundry in your house, the birth center is a great choice too. What matters most is that you feel calm and loved, and that your midwives and the people you love are there.

Do birth centers have birth pools?

Most do. You can labor in a warm birth tub, and many have big beds and room for your partner. Ask when you visit. And you can have one at home too. My midwives brought a birth pool to my house.

How long do families stay after a birth center birth?

Usually only a few hours, then you go home to rest. Ask each birth center how long families stay and when your follow-up visits are. With a home birth, you don't have to go anywhere at all.

How do I find a birth center near me?

The internet and AI are your friends here. Search online or ask an AI assistant for birth centers near you, and read what other moms say in their reviews. Then book a consultation with two or three, and ask whether they also offer home births. Not all of them do.

How do I tell my family?

If you feel weird about telling anyone, don't tell anyone. That's my biggest suggestion. You can simply say, "I have midwives, and I'm still looking at my options." That's it. If your family is supportive, absolutely tell them. For me, being pregnant made my anxiety higher, so I didn't want to be around negativity. If I noticed someone was skeptical of home birth, I didn't discuss it with them, and that's totally fine. And if someone starts telling you what they think about home birth, you can just say, "Thank you, but I'd rather not talk about it." If you do tell them, share the plan, not just the place. Tell them about the midwives who care for you for nine months, and how much they ask about you, like what you eat and how you sleep. It's a completely different kind of care. Many people don't know that, and they think a home birth is something you just wing. It's actually more like preparing for a marathon.
