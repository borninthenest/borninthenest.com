What should I ask on a birth center tour?

The short answer: The most important thing is how you feel when you walk through that door. Do you feel calm? Are you surrounded by people who are nice and kind? Can you ask any question you want? That matters most. Once that feels right, ask all your other questions.

By Sybille Mom of two · first birth in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

A birth center tour is your chance to see the rooms, meet the people and ask anything you want. Here is what's worth asking, in plain words.

What is a birth center?

A birth center is an inviting place you get to know really well, because you go there for your visits every month, so it feels a bit like a home away from home. It's beautiful and warm. Nobody rushes you, it's calm, and there are no harsh bright lights. And just like at home, you can invite whoever you want to your birth.

Who will care for me?

Your midwives. That's what it's all about. Get to know them, spend time with them, and see if they're the right fit for you. The right midwife makes you feel calm, heard and loved, right from the first moment.

How does it feel?

This part matters as much as the answers. Notice how you feel in the rooms and with the people. Do you feel calm? Heard? How a place feels to you is worth noticing. If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.

I toured three different places, maybe four. I wanted to feel each place's energy, and whether I would feel comfortable and at ease there. A space is just a space. One I really liked was all modern and nice, but the people there didn't make it special. My midwife's birth center wasn't the most modern space, but I felt so loved and welcomed there, and that's why I chose it.

Ask what they know about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing

Good pregnancy care is more than checkups. It includes how you eat, how you sleep and how you feel. Fueling your body is one of the most important things you can do during pregnancy, so your midwives should know a lot about it. If they can't really talk about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing, they're probably not the best fit.

What happens if I need a hospital?

Ask which hospital the center works with, how you would get there, who would come with you, and how you and the midwives would decide together when it's time to go. A good center answers this clearly and without hesitation. Read more about what a transfer usually looks like.

How long will I stay after the birth?

Ask how long families usually stay after the birth, what the midwives check on you and your baby before you go home, and whether anyone visits you at home or calls you in the days after. Every center does this a little differently, so it's worth hearing it from them.

What does it cost, and what's included?

Ask for the price in writing and what it covers: prenatal visits, the birth, postpartum visits and care for your baby. Ask whether the center works with your insurance, and how billing works if you transfer to a hospital. Here's more on what a home birth or birth center costs and checking your insurance.
