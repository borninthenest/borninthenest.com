What should I ask on a birth center tour?

The short answer: Ask who will care for you, whether the center is accredited and licensed, what happens if you need a hospital, how long families stay after the birth, and what the price includes. Then notice how the place and the people make you feel.

By Sybille Mom of two · first birth in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

Reviewed Nov 2, 2026 · Next review: November 2027

A birth center tour is your chance to see the rooms, meet the people and ask anything you want. Here is what's worth asking, in plain words.

What is a birth center?

The American Association of Birth Centers (AABC) describes a birth center as a freestanding, homelike facility designed in the midwifery and wellness model of pregnancy and birth, offering personalized, family-centered care for healthy women before, during and after a normal pregnancy, labor and birth. The Commission for the Accreditation of Birth Centers (CABC) adds that a freestanding birth center is not a hospital, and that birth centers are an integrated part of the health care system.

Who will care for me?

Ask which midwives attend births at the center, whether you'll get to know them during your pregnancy, and who else is on the team. AABC notes that birth center teams include certified nurse-midwives and certified professional midwives, so ask which credential each midwife holds. Here's what those letters mean.

Is the center accredited and licensed?

CABC accredits birth centers in the US. Its website lets you search for accredited centers and explains how to report a complaint about a center. Ask the center whether it's accredited, and whether it's licensed in your state. If it isn't accredited, ask why, and which standards it follows instead.

What happens if I need a hospital?

Ask which hospital the center works with, how you would get there, who would come with you, and how you and the midwives would decide together when it's time to go. A good center answers this clearly and without hesitation. Read more about what a transfer usually looks like.

How long will I stay after the birth?

Ask how long families usually stay after the birth, what the midwives check on you and your baby before you go home, and whether anyone visits you at home or calls you in the days after. Every center does this a little differently, so it's worth hearing it from them.

What does it cost, and what's included?

Ask for the price in writing and what it covers: prenatal visits, the birth, postpartum visits and care for your baby. Ask whether the center works with your insurance, and how billing works if you transfer to a hospital. Here's more on what a home birth or birth center costs and checking your insurance.

How does it feel?

This part matters as much as the answers. Notice how you feel in the rooms and with the people. Do you feel calm? Heard? A report on the hormones of birth from the National Partnership for Women & Families explains that stress hormones may slow labor when a mother doesn't feel private, calm and undisturbed. So how a place feels to you is worth noticing.

I toured three different places, maybe four. I wanted to feel each place's energy, and whether I would feel comfortable and at ease there. A space is just a space. One I really liked was all modern and nice, but the people there didn't make it special. My midwife's birth center wasn't the most modern space, but I felt so loved and welcomed there, and that's why I chose it.

Whatever you choose, talk it through with an experienced midwife and your care provider. They know you and your pregnancy, and they can help you decide.

Questions moms ask

What is an alongside midwifery unit?

CABC describes it as a midwifery-led, homelike unit located inside a hospital but separate from the labor and delivery unit, designed for women with a low-risk pregnancy who expect a normal labor and birth.

Who can give birth at a birth center?

AABC describes birth center care as care for healthy women with a normal pregnancy, labor and birth. Each center decides who it can care for, so ask about your own pregnancy.

Can my family come to the birth center?

CABC says that at a birth center, the parent's family, as they define it, is welcome to take part in the pregnancy, birth and postpartum period. Ask each center about its own guidelines.
