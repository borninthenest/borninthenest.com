When do I need to decide where to give birth? Can I switch?

The short answer: Starting early gives you the most choice, because some midwives and birth centers book up. But it's rarely too late to ask. You can switch providers during pregnancy, and some midwives welcome moms who come to them later. Call, ask what's possible, and trust your gut.

By Sybille Mom of two · first child born in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

Before my first baby, no one asked me the most important question: where do you want to give birth? I didn't even know it was a choice. No one told me I had other options until my second pregnancy. So if you're reading this in the middle of your pregnancy and wondering whether you've missed your chance, you probably haven't.

When should I start looking?

I didn't start looking for my midwife until after my first trimester, and when I met her I just had that gut feeling she was the right one.

Starting early in pregnancy gives you the most options, because popular midwives and birth centers can book up for certain months. But early isn't a deadline. If you're further along, call and ask what's possible. Here's how to find a home birth midwife.

Can I switch to a midwife later in pregnancy?

Often, yes. Some midwives welcome moms who switch later in pregnancy. Each midwife and birth center has its own rules about when they can still take someone new, and your health and your pregnancy matter too, so ask directly.

If you switch, ask your current provider for a copy of your records, so your new midwife has your full history from the start.

Can I switch from a midwife to a hospital, or back?

Yes. Plans can change, and that's okay. Sometimes it's because of your health or your baby's. Sometimes you simply feel differently than you did a few months ago. You can choose wherever you want to give birth. It's your choice. Here's how a birth center compares with a hospital.

How do I know it's time to change?

Notice how you feel after your visits. Do you feel heard? Calm? Would you keep visiting her like a friend? If something feels off, even if you can't put it into words yet, pause, write it down and ask questions. We live in a modern world with ancient instincts, so listen to yours.

My second pregnancy is when I found my midwife, and I just trusted my gut. She and her team made me feel so loved and cared for. Trusting your gut is something you may have to learn again. It's a skill many of us don't practice much.

You don't have to decide everything at once

You can choose who cares for you first and keep learning about the rest. You don't have to decide in the moment. Sit on it, breathe into it and let it settle. My own confidence wasn't there at the start. It grew over my pregnancy, with my midwives, my own reading and hypnobirthing.

Questions moms ask

Is it too late to switch to a midwife near the end of pregnancy?

It's rarely too late to ask. Some midwives can still take you late in pregnancy and some can't, so call and ask what's possible for you.

Do I have to decide where to give birth before my first prenatal visit?

No. You can start care, ask questions and decide as you learn. Starting the search early simply gives you more choice.

Can I change my mind after choosing a birth center or a home birth?

Yes. It's your pregnancy and your birth. Talk with your midwife or doctor about what changing would look like.
