My partner is worried about a home birth. What can help?

The short answer: Take the worry seriously and bring your partner into the decision. Meet your midwives together. They've done this so many times, and they can reassure your partner. Give it time. You don't have to settle it in one conversation.

By Sybille Mom of two · first child born in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

If your partner is worried about a home birth, that isn't a bad sign. Often it means they care about you and the baby, and they want to know there's a plan. My first baby was born in a hospital, and no one told me I had other options until my second pregnancy. So a partner who hesitates isn't being difficult. They may simply be hearing something new.

My partner was definitely worried. For him, the hospital was the only option and everything he had ever known, and he felt I would probably get the best care there. I kept reassuring him that this was what I wanted and that it was the right path.

Then he came to every monthly visit and met the midwives, and he felt more confident in the process than ever. He saw how much care they put into me and the baby. What I saw at the hospital was different: some quick questions about the baby and an offer of vaccines, and no questions about my nutrition, my sleep or my bowel movements. That convinced him it was the right path.

What partners often worry about

Often they're the same questions you had: What if something goes wrong? Who is in charge? How quickly can we get to a hospital? What am I supposed to do? These are good questions, and a good home birth midwife expects to answer every one of them.

Meet the midwife together

Bring your partner to the consultation and let them ask their own questions, even the ones that feel awkward. Then notice how she answers: calmly, directly, without brushing anything off.

For me, it came down to a gut feeling about my midwife, and how loved and cared for she and her team made me feel. Trusting your gut is something you may have to learn again. It's a skill many of us don't practice much. Your partner deserves the chance to get their own feeling about her, too. Here's my list of questions to ask a home birth midwife.

Talk it through together

Ask your midwife to explain her plan to both of you: which hospital, how you would get there, and what would make her recommend going.

Your midwife watches closely and will tell you if it's time to go, and you make that call with her. That can be a relief for a partner, because they don't have to judge the moment alone. I trusted that my midwives would know if I needed to transfer, so I didn't have to worry about it. Read more about what happens if you need to go to the hospital and what a home birth midwife brings.

Give your partner a real role

Knowing what to do can help a partner feel calmer. Ask your midwife what she would like your partner to help with during labor and after the birth, and practice breathing and comfort ideas together during pregnancy.

During my second birth, as the contractions got stronger, a moment of fear showed up ("I'm not a marathon runner!"), and my husband said, "You totally are. You trained for this." That was all I needed.

What helped my partner most was hearing the transfer plan, and hearing the midwives talk it through. The midwives told me they attend births at homes and birth centers regularly, and they told me they don't often have a reason to send a mom to the hospital. That's it.

Give it time

You don't have to decide in one conversation. Sit on it. Take it home, breathe into it and let it settle. If something feels off, for either of you, pause, write it down and ask more questions.

Confidence can grow over a pregnancy. Mine wasn't there at the start. It grew with my visits with my midwives, my own reading and my hypnobirthing practice.

And if the two of you decide together that a birth center or a hospital fits better, that's a good outcome too. What matters is that you both feel calm about where your baby is born.

They know you and your pregnancy, and they can help you decide.

Questions moms ask

What if my partner still doesn't want a home birth?

Keep talking, and keep asking questions together. For some couples a birth center feels like a middle path. You can also ask your midwife whether your partner can bring their questions to her directly.

Should my partner come to prenatal visits?

If they can, yes. Getting to know the midwife and her team often makes the plan feel real and much less scary.

Is it normal for partners to be scared of the birth itself?

Yes. Birth is big for partners too. Talking about it openly, and knowing what their role will be, can help.
