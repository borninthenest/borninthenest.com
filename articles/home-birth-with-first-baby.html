Can I have a home birth with my first baby?

The short answer: Many first-time moms do plan a home birth. Whether it's a good fit for you depends on your own health and your own pregnancy, so the kindest first step is a conversation with an experienced midwife. A first labor often takes its time, so it helps to prepare well and to make a plan for every path.

By Sybille Mom of two · first birth in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

I'll be honest with you from the start: my home birth was my second baby, not my first. My first baby was born in a hospital with an epidural. So I can't tell you what a first home birth feels like from my own body.

But I can tell you this. No one told me I had another option until my second pregnancy. I wish someone had, sooner. That's why I love this question so much. If you're asking it with your first baby, you're already further along than I was.

Yes, it's a real option

A planned home birth is a recognized choice in the US. ACOG, the organization of US obstetricians, says you have the right to choose where you give birth, and encourages you to learn about each option first. In 2024, about 1.6% of US babies were born at home, which was 58,626 babies (CDC final birth data). Some of those were first babies, and their moms asked the very same question you're asking now.

Whether it's right for you is a different question, and it's a personal one. It depends on your health, your pregnancy, where you live and what feels right in your heart.

What's a little different with a first baby

Everything is new the first time. Your body hasn't done this before, and you haven't either. A first labor often takes its time, so plans can change along the way, and that's okay.

That's why a good midwife will talk with you early about every path: staying home, and what would happen if you'd like or need to go to the hospital. It's also why some first-time moms love the idea of a birth center, a calm, home-like middle path. Both are good choices, and you don't have to decide on day one.

How I'd prepare, if I were you

My confidence didn't come all at once. At the start of my second pregnancy, I didn't have it yet. It grew month by month, through monthly visits with my midwives, then weekly ones near the end, books and hypnobirthing practice. By month eight, I trusted my body more than I ever had.

If I were a first-time mom thinking about home birth, I'd start in the same place:

  • Meet one or two midwives early. Many offer a free consultation. Here are the questions I'd ask.
  • Learn how birth really works. A good childbirth class, a few calm books and some hypnobirthing practice can turn the scary movie version into something you understand.
  • Bring your partner along. It's their first birth too, and they'll feel calmer if they know the plan.
  • Make a plan for every path. Ask your midwife what happens if you need to go to the hospital, so that part feels known instead of scary.

Is it safe?

Every mom should ask this question, and I'm so glad you're asking it. The honest answer depends on your own health and your own pregnancy, so it's different for every one of us. An experienced midwife will look at your whole picture with you and tell you honestly whether home birth is a good fit for you. For me, with my midwives, I felt safe, cared for and loved.

Whatever you choose, talk it through with an experienced midwife and your care provider. They know you and your pregnancy, and they're the right people to help you decide.

Questions moms ask

Is it safe to have a home birth with my first baby?

It's a question every mom should ask. The honest answer depends on your own health and your own pregnancy, so it's different for every one of us. An experienced midwife will look at your whole picture with you and tell you honestly whether home birth is a good fit for you. For me, with my midwives, I felt safe, cared for and loved.

Do midwives take first-time moms for home births?

Many do. Each midwife has her own guidelines and each state has its own rules, so ask her directly at your first consultation.

Should I choose a birth center instead for my first baby?

Some first-time moms love a birth center as a calm, home-like middle path, and some feel most relaxed at home. There's no one right answer. Visit, ask questions and notice where you feel most at ease.

When should I start planning?

Early in pregnancy works well, because you'll have time to get to know your midwife. But it's rarely too late to ask. Some midwives welcome moms who switch later in pregnancy.

Can I change my mind later?

Yes. You can change providers or plans during pregnancy, and you can ask for an epidural in labor if you want one. Choosing a plan isn't a promise you have to keep no matter what.
