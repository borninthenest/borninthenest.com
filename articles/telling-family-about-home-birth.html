How do I tell my family I'm planning a home birth?

The short answer: You don't have to tell anyone before the birth. If you feel weird about telling anyone, don't tell anyone. If you do share, tell them about the plan, not just the place: who your midwife is. Listen to their worry, and remember you don't owe anyone a debate.

By Sybille Mom of two · first child born in a hospital, second at home · Education only, not medical advice

Telling your family can feel harder than making the decision.

It's okay not to tell anyone until after

I didn't want the worry on me. In my experience, if your friends or family probably won't support it, mentioning it doesn't help. They may just worry, and that worry can pull you into a negative space.

It's okay if waiting feels right for you, and it's also okay to share. You don't owe anyone your birth plans. Pregnancy brings many decisions, and you don't have to make them in the moment. Sit on it, and share when you're ready, whether that's early, after you've met your midwife, or after your baby is here.

Telling people after the birth

If you wait, you get to share the news in your own words, with your baby already in your arms.

If you do tell them: share the plan, not just the place

"Home birth" on its own can sound like "no plan." What often helps is describing the care around it: who your midwife is, how often you see her, what she brings to the birth, and that she has a plan with a nearby hospital in case you need to go.

If they want to read more, you can point them to what happens if you need to go to the hospital, what a midwife brings or the birth options guide.

When someone reacts with fear

Some people have never known anyone who planned a home birth, so the first reaction might be surprise or fear. That reaction often comes from what they know, not from anything about you.

Listen first. Then answer the question underneath, which is often some version of "what if something goes wrong?" You can explain that your midwife watches closely, that she will tell you if it's time to go and you make that call with her, and that hospitals are there when more help is needed.

You can also set a boundary. It's your pregnancy and your birth. It's okay to tell people kindly that you've made your choice with your midwife and that what you need from them now is support, not a debate.

Protect your calm

Feeling calm matters in labor. Stress hormones are your body's way of protecting you. In nature, they would slow labor down if there was danger nearby. So lean on the people who make you feel loved and cared for, and give yourself permission to keep some conversations short, or to skip them for now.

If a worried relative has also scared you, write the worry down and bring it to your next visit. Your midwife can answer it for you, and you don't have to carry it alone. That's what I did, and they always had a good answer to ease my worries.

Will older children be at the birth?

Some families want older children at home during the birth. Others arrange for someone to look after them, at home or somewhere else. There's no single right answer.

If your children will be at home, it helps to have one trusted adult whose only job is to look after them, so your partner can stay with you.

At my home birth, my older child was there until bedtime. I wanted her there so she could see how amazing a birth is and that it's nothing to be afraid of. I wanted it to empower her. It's a very personal decision, but I'd encourage every mom to have her children there if they want to be, and to explain beforehand what the birth will look like. I told my child that mommy would do a lot of breathing, be very quiet and sometimes really loud.

I brought her to every checkup, and the midwives even let her check the baby's heartbeat. They involved her in every step, which I think is the best thing. She truly wanted to be there, until it was time to sleep.

Questions moms ask

Do I have to tell my family I'm planning a home birth?

No. If you feel weird about telling anyone, don't tell anyone. It's your choice whether, when and how much to share.

What if my family is against home birth?

Listen to their worry. Tell them about the plan: that in the rare case you need a transfer, your midwives know if something is off and have a plan to get you to the hospital. If you're comfortable, invite them to bring their questions to your midwife. Then let it rest. You don't have to win the conversation.

Can older children be at a home birth?

Absolutely. It's your choice. I wanted mine to be there. If they will be there, plan for a trusted adult to look after them.
