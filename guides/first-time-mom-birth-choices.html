First-time mom? What no one tells you about your birth choices

The things I wish I'd known before my first baby.

My first baby was born in a hospital, the way most of us are told it should go. I was induced early and had an epidural. I was completely numb, and I felt helpless. No one ever asked me the most important question: where do you want to give birth? I didn't even know it was a choice. No one told me I had other options until my second pregnancy. I want you to know sooner.

1. You get to choose where you give birth

In the US, you have many options: you can plan a birth at home with a midwife, in a birth center or in a hospital. You can choose wherever you want to give birth. It's your choice. Read the overview of your birth options.

2. Your feelings matter to your body

When you feel calm and loved, your body can relax and let labor flow. That's why choosing where to give birth isn't only a medical question. It's also about where you feel most at ease, so your body can do what it was made to do.

3. Midwives aren't only for home births

Yes, you can have a midwife at home, in a birth center or in a hospital. Many families never meet a midwife simply because no one suggested it.

4. A birth center can be a beautiful choice for a first baby

A birth center is a home-like, midwife-led space with comfy beds and birth tubs, and for many first-time moms it feels like a calm middle path between home and hospital. Every pregnancy is different.

5. First labors are often longer, and that's okay

A long first labor doesn't mean you have to go to a hospital. It means it's even more important to find a place where you feel calm and loved. When you don't feel that way, labor can slow down or even stop, and that's when things can get complicated.

6. You can change your mind, even later in pregnancy.

The first doctor or midwife you see doesn't have to be the one who's with you at your birth. If something doesn't feel right, you can switch, even later in your pregnancy. It's your pregnancy and your birth.

Wondering if it's too late to change? Read when to decide where to give birth, and how to switch.
