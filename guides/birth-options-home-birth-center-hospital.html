Your birth options in the US: home, birth center or hospital

Three places, three kinds of care, and one question to help you choose.

Before my first birth, no one ever asked me where I wanted to give birth. I didn't even know it was a choice. Here's what I wish someone had told me: in the US you can plan to give birth at home, in a birth center or in a hospital, and you have the right to choose.

🏡 Home birth

At a planned home birth, a midwife cares for you through pregnancy, comes to you when labor begins, and stays with you through the birth and the first hours after. Prenatal visits are often long and unhurried, sometimes in your own living room. In labor you can move, eat, rest, use a birth pool and be surrounded only by the people you choose.

Midwives bring equipment to listen to your baby, check your blood pressure, help with bleeding and help a baby breathe if needed, and they plan a route to a hospital from the very first visit. After the birth, your midwives come back to check on you and your baby the next day, right in your own bed. They come to your house, which is amazing. Of course you can have a home birth with your first baby. Many first-time moms do, and your midwives have cared for many, many first-time moms before you.

🌿 Birth center

A birth center is an inviting place, and one you get to know really well, because you go there for your visits every month. So it feels a bit like a home away from home. That's what I love about birth centers. They're beautiful and warm. Nobody rushes you, it's calm, and there are no harsh bright lights. And just like at home, you can invite whoever you want to your birth.

🏥 Hospital

Hospitals are also an okay place to give birth, and they're a wonderful option if something doesn't go as planned. Many offer midwife care, and you can still bring calm with you, like dim lights and your own music. There are more rules and restrictions, and it's not what I would choose for myself, but if a hospital is where you feel most relaxed, that's wonderful.

How to choose

  1. Picture your birth. Close your eyes and imagine it. Who's there? What room are you in? How does it make you feel?
  2. Talk to different people and visit different places. Talk to midwives and doctors, visit a birth center and visit a hospital, and ask moms who have given birth in different places. The internet and AI are your friends here. Search online or ask an AI assistant for midwives and birth centers near you, and read their reviews. Everywhere you go, ask yourself: do I feel calm and loved here? If you do, wonderful, then that's a great place for you. If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.
  3. Choose your team, and take your time. When you find the midwives or doctors who make you feel calm and loved, that's your team. You wouldn't run a marathon without knowing your team, and birth is the same: you want people who know you and your baby inside and out. There's no rush. You can even start with a hospital and switch later if it doesn't feel right.

Your first steps

  1. Find a midwife, birth center or hospital. The internet and AI are your friends here. Search online or ask an AI assistant for midwives, birth centers and hospitals near you, and read what other moms say in their reviews.
  2. Book a consultation with two or three. Most offer one.
  3. Notice how you feel. Pay attention to your very first feelings when you walk in, and to the people you meet and talk with. Do you feel loved, calm and heard? If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.
  4. Ask your questions about training, backup, transfer plan, equipment, cost, and what they know about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing.
