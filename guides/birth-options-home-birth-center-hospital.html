Your birth options in the US: home, birth center or hospital

Three places, three kinds of care, and one gentle question to help you choose.

Before my first birth, no one ever asked me where I wanted to give birth. I didn't even know it was a choice. Here's what I wish someone had told me: in the US you can plan to give birth at home, in a birth center or in a hospital, and you have the right to choose.

🏡 Home birth

At a planned home birth, a midwife cares for you through pregnancy, comes to you when labor begins, and stays with you through the birth and the first hours after. Prenatal visits are often long and unhurried, sometimes in your own living room. In labor you can move, eat, rest, use a birth pool and be surrounded only by the people you choose.

Midwives bring equipment to listen to your baby, check your blood pressure, help with bleeding and help a baby breathe if needed, and they plan a route to a hospital from the very first visit. For healthy, low-risk pregnancies, large studies have found fewer interventions at home, such as inductions, episiotomies and cesareans. One honest note for first-time moms: in a large study from England, planned home birth for a first baby came with a small increase in serious problems for babies, about 4 more per 1,000 births.

🌿 Birth center

A birth center is a freestanding, home-like place run by midwives. The American Association of Birth Centers calls it "a maximized home rather than a mini-hospital," with comfy beds, birth tubs and family welcome, plus arrangements for transfer to a hospital when needed.

It's a beautiful middle path, and a lovely choice for a first baby. In England's large Birthplace study, first-time moms who planned to give birth in midwife-led units had the same outcomes for their babies as in hospital, with fewer interventions. In a large US study of 79 birth centers, 84 in 100 women gave birth at the center and 6 in 100 had a cesarean.

🏥 Hospital

A hospital is the right place for higher-risk pregnancies, for twins or a baby who isn't head-down, and if you know you'd like an epidural. I'm grateful hospitals exist for when something needs more help. Many hospitals offer midwife care too, and you can still bring calm with you: dim lights, your own music, your breathing and the people you love.

How to choose, gently

Close your eyes for a moment and ask yourself: where would I feel safest, calmest and most loved? Your body does its best work where you can relax. Then bring that answer to the practical questions:

  • Is my pregnancy healthy and low-risk? Your provider can tell you, and it can change, so keep asking.
  • How far is the nearest hospital, and what is the midwife's transfer plan?
  • Which midwives and birth centers are near me, and what does my insurance cover?
  • Who do I want in the room with me?

Your first steps

  1. Find a midwife or birth center. Try the ACNM Find a Midwife search, NARM for certified professional midwives, and the AABC birth center directory.
  2. Book a free consultation with one or two. Most offer one.
  3. Ask your questions about training, backup, transfer plan, equipment and cost.
  4. Notice how you feel afterward. Calm and heard is a very good sign.
