Home birth, honestly

A few myths, gently untangled.

MythHome birth is only for very, very crunchy people.

FactIt's a recognized choice. ACOG, the organization of US obstetricians, says you have the right to choose where you give birth, and more than 58,000 US babies were born at home in 2024 (CDC final birth data).

MythAt home, you're on your own if plans change.

FactA planned home birth is made with a qualified midwife and a hospital transfer plan arranged before labor, as ACOG recommends. In a US study of 16,924 planned home births, about 9 in 10 moms gave birth at home, and most moves to the hospital were for a long, slow labor.

MythWithout a hospital, there's nothing for the pain.

FactMany midwives bring a birth pool, so warm water is one comfort option at home. Some moms want stronger relief than home can offer, and that's okay too. And you don't have to walk in cold on the day. My midwives helped me prepare all through my pregnancy, the way an Olympian trains for a race. For me, that changed everything: it was pain, but not suffering.

Why so many moms love it

  • Your own nest. Your bed, your people, your snacks, your playlist.
  • A midwife who knows you. With continuity of midwife care, you see the same midwife, or the same small team, through pregnancy, birth and the weeks after.

Home birth is planned for healthy, low-risk pregnancies, and it isn't right for every pregnancy. ACOG recommends against it with twins, a baby who isn't head-down, or after a previous cesarean. But ask before you rule it out: an experienced midwife or doctor who knows you can help you find what fits. When a home birth might still be possible →

The birth story you'll want to tell.

With my first baby, I had an epidural in the hospital. I was completely numb, and I felt helpless. I didn't know what I didn't know. I had no idea what labor really feels like, so I just assumed it was supposed to be scary and a little insane.

My second birth, at home in my own bed, was a completely different story. I'd spent about four months practicing my hypnobirthing breathing, so I knew what was coming, and I wasn't scared. I got a real break between every contraction, a moment to breathe and rest, like I'd done it a hundred times already. My midwife always told me to get comfortable with the contractions on my own first, and call after that. It all felt so calm and manageable that I didn't think I was in real labor yet. I'd practiced it over and over again, and I felt so in control that I told my husband I was totally fine without the midwives. Which is crazy, I know.

Then the pushing sensation came, and I realized: oh, this is it. Luckily, Sarah knew better. She'd pushed back: "Nope, I'm coming over now." She walked in around 8:30, right as I started pushing and the contractions got really strong, and about an hour later my second child was born. I remember thinking, wow, this is really cool. My body was made for this. I just had to let it.

And then she was here. Pure joy. 🪺
