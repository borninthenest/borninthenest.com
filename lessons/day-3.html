My two births, and what changed

One in a hospital, one at home. Here's what I learned.

Today I want to share something personal: my two births. Every birth is different, and mine isn't a rulebook. But stories have a way of making things feel possible.

My first baby: the hospital

With my first baby, I was scared. I thought I had to give birth in a hospital, and I had no idea what to expect. My labor lasted nearly two and a half days. Along the way I felt miserable and lost, because I had no control over what my body wanted to do. And after I gave birth, I was drained, but they still wouldn't feed me, and they kept asking me questions instead of letting me rest.

My second baby: at home

My second birth was a completely different story. I found the most amazing midwives, and that changed everything. I felt truly loved and cared for by these women, and they made me feel so confident that my body was ready to do this. I read a couple of books, practiced hypnobirthing, and met with them every month. Their words, their calm and their expertise prepared me for my home birth, and it became the most beautiful thing I could ever imagine.

On the day, I felt so calm and in control. It was so manageable that I told my midwife she didn't need to come over yet. Thankfully, she came anyway. At 8:30 she and another midwife were at my house, and an hour later my baby was here. It felt like five minutes.

My contractions got stronger, and I moved between sitting and standing. Then the urge to push started, and I just let my body do it. You always get a break in between. The ring of fire was real, but it lasted only a few seconds. I breathed through it, and it didn't even matter, because seconds later my baby arrived. Her eyes were so wide open, looking around the room, so awake and so beautiful.

I held her in my arms and nursed her, and we waited until the cord stopped pulsing before it was cut. It felt so amazing. There was no rushing. Then I ate a massive meal and a banana, because I was really hungry. And then I got to sleep in my own bed, in my own house, with my own family. This was the golden hour I never got at the hospital. It was the most beautiful time of my life, and that's why I started Born in the Nest: so you can experience it too.

If you asked me today, "Do you want to give birth right now?" I'd say, "Yeah, sure." It was that manageable for me, because I felt so prepared. That's what I want for you too.

What I'd tell my first-time self

If something feels off, even if you can't put it into words yet, pause. Write it down. Ask questions. There's almost always another way. We live in a modern world with ancient instincts, so listen to yours. ❤️

I'm sharing this not to say a hospital is wrong. For some women and some pregnancies, it's exactly the right place, and I'm grateful it exists. I'm sharing it because no one told me I had another option until my second pregnancy. I want you to know sooner.

More stories are coming. In the weekly Born in the Nest letter, I'll share calm, real stories from other mothers: home births, birth center births, and births where plans changed and still felt good. Stories that help you feel braver.

Have a story to share? If you've given birth at home or in a birth center and would like to help another mom feel braver, just write to me at hello@borninthenest.com. I'll always ask your permission before sharing anything, and I can keep your name private.

Sybille 🤍
