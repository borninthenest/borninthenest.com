The question no one asked me while pregnant with my first child

A simple comparison, the questions to ask, and what comes next.

While pregnant with my first child, no one ever asked me the most important question: Where do you want to give birth? I didn't even know it was a choice.

You made it to Day 5! Here's the big idea one more time: you have a choice, and your body does its best work where you feel calm and loved.

Your three options, simply

🏡 Home birth: your own bed, your own space, a midwife who knows you, and the people you love right beside you. In the rare case you need a transfer, your midwife has a plan, but most of the time you get to stay right where you are, at home. Your team knows you and your baby inside and out, and they're with you during the birth.

🌿 Birth center: a calm, home-like place made just for giving birth, with comfy beds, birth tubs and midwives who know you. Your family is welcome, and you can move, eat and rest the way your body wants. Your team knows you and your baby inside and out, and they're with you during the birth.

🏥 Hospital: if a hospital is the place where you feel most relaxed, that's wonderful, go there. If you're not sure yet, take your time, meet a few midwives, and see what feels right before you decide. Staff work in shifts, so you'll likely meet completely different people, and it may not be your own doctor.

Your first steps

  1. Find a midwife, birth center or hospital. The internet and AI are your friends here. Search online or ask an AI assistant for midwives, birth centers and hospitals near you, and read what other moms say in their reviews.
  2. Book a consultation with two or three. Most offer one.
  3. Notice how you feel. Pay attention to your very first feelings when you walk in, and to the people you meet and talk with. Do you feel loved, calm and heard? If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.
  4. Ask your questions.
    • Notice how you feel. This is the most important one. When you take your first step into the birth center or the office, do you feel calm? Do you feel like you'd love to hang out there and just be around your midwives, without even having to talk? That's the feeling we're going for. Then you can ask all the other questions.
    • Ask what they know about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing. Good pregnancy care is more than checkups. It includes how you eat, how you sleep and how you feel. Fueling your body is one of the most important things you can do during pregnancy, so your midwives should know a lot about it. If they can't really talk about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing, they're probably not the best fit.
    • How many births have you attended, and what's your training?
    • How do you prepare women for birth?
    • What nutrition and lifestyle advice do you give during pregnancy?
    • What does labor actually feel like when I'm not numb?
    • Who's your backup, and who comes with you to births?
    • What equipment and medicines do you bring?
    • How will my partner be part of it?
    • What does it cost, and does my insurance cover a birth center or home birth?
    • Do I have to decide right now, or when?

When you're ready to read more: Here are my favorite books, the ones that truly helped me. I love them, and I'll read them again. Ina May's Guide to Childbirth and Spiritual Midwifery by Ina May Gaskin (her famous reminder: "your body is not a lemon"), HypnoBirthing: The Mongan Method by Marie Mongan, and The Heart in the Womb by Dr. Amali Lokugamage, an obstetrician's look at love and birth. Ina May's books include many detailed birth stories, so skip any that feel like too much.

Short on time? I've got you. I'll be doing a deep dive into each of these books in my weekly letter, with the most important takeaways, in case you don't have time to read them all.

With love from my nest to yours,
Sybille 🤍
