The question no one asked me before my first birth

A simple comparison, the questions to ask, and what comes next.

Before my first birth, no one ever asked me the most important question: Where do you want to give birth? I didn't even know it was a choice.

You made it to Day 5! Here's the big idea one more time: you have a choice, and your body does its best work where you feel calm and loved.

Your three options, simply

🏡 Home birth: your own space, a midwife you know, and the people you love around you. It's planned for healthy, low-risk pregnancies, with a qualified midwife and a clear transfer plan, and it usually involves fewer interventions like inductions, episiotomies and cesareans. Every pregnancy is different, so talk your options through with an experienced midwife or doctor who knows you.

🌿 Birth center: a home-like, midwife-led space outside the hospital, with comfy beds and birth tubs. It's a calm middle path and a good choice for a first baby. Midwives lead your care there, and your family is welcome.

🏥 Hospital: the right place for higher-risk pregnancies, or if you know you'd like an epidural. Many hospitals have midwives on the team too, and you can bring calm with you: dim lights, your own music, and your breathing.

Your first gentle steps

  1. Find a midwife. You'll see two main types: CNMs (certified nurse-midwives, licensed in every state) and CPMs (certified professional midwives, who specialize in out-of-hospital birth; licensing varies by state). Try the ACNM, NARM, and American Association of Birth Centers directories.
  2. Book a free consultation with one or two. Most offer one.
  3. Ask these questions:
    • How many births have you attended, and what's your training?
    • How do you prepare women for birth?
    • What nutrition and lifestyle advice do you give during pregnancy?
    • What does labor actually feel like when I'm not numb?
    • Who's your backup, and who comes with you to births?
    • What happens if I need to transfer to a hospital, and which hospital do you use?
    • What equipment and medicines do you bring?
    • How will my partner be part of it?
    • What does it cost, and does my insurance cover a birth center or home birth?
    • Do I have to decide right now, or when?
  4. Notice how you feel after talking with her. How do you know a midwife or birth center feels right for you? You feel calm, heard and loved, and your gut says yes.

When you're ready to read more: Ina May's Guide to Childbirth and Spiritual Midwifery by Ina May Gaskin (her famous reminder: "your body is not a lemon"), HypnoBirthing: The Mongan Method by Marie Mongan, and The Heart in the Womb by Dr. Amali Lokugamage, an obstetrician's gentle look at love and birth. Ina May's books include many detailed birth stories, so skip any that feel like too much.

Short on time? I've got you. I'll be doing a deep dive into each of these books in my weekly letter, with the most important takeaways, in case you don't have time to read them all.
