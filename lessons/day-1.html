Where would you feel calmest giving birth?

One question to start with, and what my own answer looked like.

Home birth, explained by a mom who's done it. That's what these five short lessons are, right here on the site, no signup needed.

In the movies, birth is a woman screaming in pain while everyone rushes around her. Most of us have watched that scene a hundred times, and somewhere along the way we started believing it.

But what if birth doesn't have to be like that? Yes, there's intensity, and there can be pain. But pain and suffering aren't the same thing. Birth can be one of the most beautiful, powerful things you'll ever do. We've just been taught to see it through the wrong lens.

I'm Sybille, a mom of two. My first baby was born in a hospital with an epidural. I was completely numb, and I felt helpless. My second was born at home, in my own bed, with a midwife I trusted, and that birth showed me how calm and beautiful giving birth can be. Same body, completely different birth. The biggest difference was where I was and how calm and loved I felt.

As my midwife Sarah always said, pregnant women need to feel loved. That's what Born in the Nest is about.

Hospitals do beautiful, important work, and for some pregnancies they're exactly the right place. And for many of us, birth is also something our bodies were made to do.

So let's start with one question.

Close your eyes for a moment. Where would you feel calmest, most at ease, and most loved?

There's no wrong answer. For some women, it's a hospital with a team they trust. For many of us, the honest answer is somewhere familiar and quiet, with people we love. Whatever yours is, take it seriously when you choose where to give birth.

My answer, the second time. At home, I could dim the lights. I had mood lighting and my own essential oils. When labor got going, I wanted darkness. I chose the smallest bathroom in the house and stayed there until it was pushing time and I wanted to lie down.

With my first baby, in the hospital, some bright lights had to stay on, and deep in contractions every bit of light bothered me. That's not a criticism of the hospital. In labor, I just wanted a dark, quiet nook.

Getting ready for it. My midwives prepared me through my whole pregnancy, the way an Olympian trains for a race. For months, I practiced breathing through each wave. So when labor came, I met it with a different mindset. For me, it was pain, but not suffering.

So when you think about where to give birth, it's not only a medical question. It's also where you'd feel calmest, and who you'd want next to you. That's a good conversation to start with your midwife or doctor.

A tiny practice for today: take three slow breaths, longer on the out-breath than the in-breath, and notice your shoulders drop. Then picture the room you'd want to labor in. How bright is it? Who's there?
