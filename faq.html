What are my options for where to give birth in the US?

You can plan to give birth at home with a midwife, in a freestanding birth center, or in a hospital. You can choose wherever you want to give birth. It's your choice.

Most US babies are born in hospitals, so if you've never heard much about the other two options, you're not alone.

What are the alternatives to a hospital birth?

The two main alternatives are a planned home birth and a birth center birth. Both are usually led by midwives, and planned with a clear path to a hospital if more help is needed.

A birth center is an inviting place you get to know really well, because you go there for your visits every month, so it feels a bit like a home away from home. It's beautiful and warm. Nobody rushes you, it's calm, and there are no harsh bright lights. And just like at home, you can invite whoever you want to your birth. If a hospital is the right place for you, many hospitals also offer midwife care.

What should I know about home birth?

Home birth is an amazing option. Before you rule it out, go talk to midwives and other moms who have actually done it, and see if it's the right fit for you. For me, after giving birth in a hospital, I knew there had to be another option, because it just didn't feel like the right fit. A birth center and my midwives were one hundred percent the right fit. Every time I walked in, I felt cared for and calm, instead of rushed like in a busy hospital.

What should I know about home birth with a first baby?

With a first baby there's so much that's new. Many first-time moms also love the idea of a birth center, an inviting place you get to know well.

A first labor may take time, which is why it's even more important to be somewhere you feel calm and loved, so you don't slow the process down. That's one reason a birth center or your own home is a beautiful choice. Your midwife can help you weigh what's best for you and make the decision that feels right.

Home birth vs birth center: what's the difference?

With a home birth, your midwife comes to you. You labor in your own space, in your own bed or a birth pool, with only the people you choose. With a birth center, you go to an inviting place run by midwives, often with large beds and birth tubs.

At home, everything happens on your terms: your own lights, your own food, your own space. In a hospital, things mostly happen on their terms.

Both are midwife-led. Choosing between them often comes down to where you feel most relaxed, how far you live from a hospital, and what's available near you.

Who is a good candidate for a home birth or birth center?

Any mom can be a fit for a home birth or a birth center. The only moms who aren't are the ones who want to give birth in a hospital no matter what. If you're curious, go talk to midwives who attend births at home or in a birth center and see if it feels right for you.

What happens if I need to go to the hospital during labor?

In the rare case you need a transfer, just talk to your midwife. She'll have a plan, and she'll come with you, and that's the beautiful thing. Your midwives will know if anything is out of the norm, and they'll have a plan to get you to the hospital. That's how I felt with mine.

My partner is worried about home birth. What can help?

Most of the time the worry isn't really about how you'd get to the hospital. It's about seeing that birth is the most natural process, and women have been doing it for centuries. Meet your midwives together. They've done this so many times, and they can reassure your partner that there's nothing to be worried about.

How do I tell my family I'm planning a home birth?

You don't have to tell anyone before the birth. If you feel weird about telling anyone, don't tell anyone. If you do share, tell them about the plan, not just the place: who your midwife is. Listen to their worry, and remember you don't owe anyone a debate.

Who can be at my home birth?

Really anyone you want. Your partner, your other kids, your mom, your best friend. It's your birth, so you decide. Your older children can absolutely be part of it, which is so amazing, because that's something you usually can't have at a hospital. Some people even make it a party.

How do I find a midwife?

The easiest way is to search online for birth centers near you and midwives near you. The internet and AI are your friends here. Search online or ask an AI assistant for midwives, birth centers and hospitals near you, and read what other moms say in their reviews. I think those matter a lot, because real moms are sharing how it really went. Directories are a second option, but honestly they're so-so. Trust what other moms say.

Then book a consultation with two or three. Most offer one. Ask about her training and experience, who her backup is, what equipment and medicines she brings, and what it costs. Then notice how you feel after talking with her. Do you feel loved, calm and heard? If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.

When do I need to decide where to give birth?

Starting early gives you the most choice, because some midwives and birth centers book up. But it's rarely too late to ask, and you can switch providers during pregnancy. Call, ask what's possible, and trust your gut.

What if I go past my due date?

My midwives could not care less about my due date. From the very beginning they told me the baby comes when the baby comes. So you have a birth month, but the exact day doesn't really matter. Hospitals often treat that date like a ticking clock. As you get close, they want you to come in, and if you go a day past it, everyone gets worried, and that can make you nervous too. With midwives, you just wait for your baby.

What should I ask on a birth center tour?

The most important thing is how you feel when you walk through that door. Do you feel calm? Are you surrounded by people who are nice and kind? Can you ask any question you want? That matters most. Once that feels right, ask all your other questions. If you can't be yourself, can't ask any question you want, or feel pushed into certain things, that's a red flag, and you should pay attention to it.

Ask who will care for you, what happens if you need a hospital, how long families stay after the birth, and what the price includes. Ask what they know about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing. Good pregnancy care is more than checkups. It includes how you eat, how you sleep and how you feel. Fueling your body is one of the most important things you can do during pregnancy, so your midwives should know a lot about it. If they can't really talk about nutrition, sleep and wellbeing, they're probably not the best fit.

How much does a home birth cost?

I paid $5,600 to my birth center midwives. I went there for all my visits, and my baby was born at home, because that's where I wanted to give birth. From what I saw, that's about average. Some midwives charged a little more and some a little less. It's usually one package fee that covers your prenatal visits, the birth and your care afterward. What you actually pay depends on your insurance, and many midwives will help you check your coverage, so it's a great question for your first visit. A hospital birth can cost a lot more or less depending on your plan. It depends on your insurance either way, so ask your insurer and each place for their prices.

Many places offer payment plans and loans, and they work with your insurance. You can often use your health savings account (HSA), and there may be tax deductions too, so really, all options are open.

I'm scared of giving birth. Is that normal?

Yes, it's very normal, and you're not alone. Fear of birth is so common because we grew up believing birth is scary. It is not. Your body is amazing, and it knows how to do this.

What helps: learning how birth really works, not the movie version. Labor comes in waves, and between contractions you get real breaks to rest and breathe. Nature doesn't want you to suffer. Knowing your options, a midwife or doctor who helps you feel calm, heard, and loved, and practicing calm breathing also help. If the fear is weighing on you, please tell your provider.

What does continuity of midwife care mean?

It means you're building your team. Think of it like a marathon: you assemble your team ahead of time, and on the day that matters, they're right there with you, and you trust them with everything. Imagine running a marathon without even knowing your team. You'd feel out of place. That's kind of how it felt for me at the hospital. With my home birth it was the opposite. I knew my team inside and out, and they knew me and my baby inside and out.

Can I use a birth pool or warm water in labor?

Warm water, such as a birth pool, tub or shower, is one comfort option many midwives and birth centers offer. Ask your midwife or birth center about birth pools and tubs. And you can have one at home too. My midwives brought a birth pool to my house.

What is hypnobirthing?

Hypnobirthing is a childbirth education approach that uses deep relaxation, slow breathing and visualization to help you stay calm in labor. When you're scared, your body tenses up, and that makes labor harder. When you feel calm and cared for, your body can relax and do its work. The best-known method is HypnoBirthing: The Mongan Method by Marie Mongan.
