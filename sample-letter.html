Halfway there 🌸

What a weekly letter looks like, written for around weeks 20–24.

A sample letter. Every Sunday's letter is matched to where you are in your pregnancy, and this one is written for around weeks 20–24.

SubjectHalfway there 🌸 (and a word about marathons)

Hi there,

You're around weeks 20–24 now. Halfway! 🌸 In case no one has told you yet this week: you're doing beautifully.

🏡 One honest moment

When my second child was being born, a moment of fear showed up, and I blurted out, "I'm not a marathon runner!"

My husband said, "You totally are. You trained for this."

He was right, in the gentlest way. I've never run a marathon. But for about four months before my second child was born, I practiced breathing through each wave. Small, quiet practice, again and again, until my body knew it by heart. When the big day came, it was there for me.

So this letter is about training. The soft kind. No running shoes required.

🌱 What's happening around now

  • Little flutters. Many women start to feel their baby move somewhere around now: tiny bubbles, taps or flutters. With a first baby it often comes a little later, and every pregnancy has its own timing.
  • The anatomy scan. Many women are offered a detailed ultrasound, often called the anatomy scan, somewhere around weeks 18–22. It can feel exciting and a little nerve-racking at the same time. Both are okay.
  • A bit more energy. Many women find this middle stretch of pregnancy brings some energy back. If that's you, enjoy it. If it isn't, rest is part of the training too.

💛 What to focus on this week

  1. Two minutes of breathing. Tonight before sleep, breathe in for 4 and out for 6, and let your jaw go loose. That's it. You're not doing it for tonight. You're doing it for the day you meet your baby.
  2. Fuel and move in a way you enjoy. The day my second child was born looked completely ordinary: coffee, my weekly 45-minute walk, lunch, my daily stretches. (And a steak dinner. About five minutes later it was go time. Zero regrets. 🥩) Ask your midwife or doctor what movement and food look like for your pregnancy, then find a version that feels good.

🩺 Questions worth asking your midwife or doctor

  • What will you look at during my anatomy scan, and what happens next if anything needs a closer look?
  • What kind of movement and exercise feels right for my pregnancy right now?
  • What does good fuel look like for me these days, and is there anything you'd like me to keep an eye on?
  • When should I start practicing breathing or relaxation for birth, and are there classes you'd recommend?

My midwives paid attention to everything: what I was eating, how I was sleeping, how I was really feeling. So bring all of it to your next visit. And the advice I'd give my first-time self: if something feels off, even if you can't put it into words yet, pause. Write it down. Ask questions.

With love from my nest to yours,
Sybille 🤍
